AI can draft a message in seconds. It cannot decide what that message means in a relationship, a workplace, or a culture.

<div class="callout" role="note" aria-label="Project goal">
This primer asks a simple question: **when an AI helps write the message, who is responsible for the communication?**
</div>

## The problem: a polished message that lands badly

Imagine that a manager asks an AI tool to make feedback “clearer and more professional.” The result is grammatically smooth, but the employee reads it as cold, threatening, or unfair. The sender says, “That is not what I meant.” The employee replies, “But that is what you said.”

This is not only a writing problem. It is a communication problem involving channels, signs, interpretation, culture, and power.

## The promise

The goal is to make the commonplace visible. You do not need a communication degree to use theory: each lens gives you a different question to ask before a small misunderstanding becomes a larger conflict.

<p class="lead">Read the case first, then use the lenses as a diagnostic toolkit—not as a formula for making every message sound the same.</p>

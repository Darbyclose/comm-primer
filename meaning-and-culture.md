  <div class="kicker">MEANING & CULTURE / 03</div>

  <h2>Same words. Different meanings.</h2>

  <p>Have you ever sent someone a text that you thought was completely normal, only for them to take it the wrong way? Maybe you were joking, but they thought you were being serious. Or maybe someone replied with just "okay." and you spent the next ten minutes wondering if they were mad at you.</p>

  <p>That's what makes communication interesting. Words don't always mean the same thing to everyone, and a lot of what we understand comes from context, relationships, and our own experiences.</p>

  <h2>So, what is semiotics?</h2>

  <p>Semiotics is the study of signs and how they create meaning. A sign can be a word, an image, an emoji, a sound, or even punctuation.</p>

  <p>Daniel Chandler explains that we interpret signs through social and cultural codes. Basically, we learn what things mean from the world around us, and not everyone learns those meanings in exactly the same way.</p>

  <p>Think about a heart emoji. Depending on who sends it, it could mean love, friendship, appreciation, or just that someone liked your message. The emoji itself doesn't change, but its meaning does.</p>

  <h2>What does this have to do with AI?</h2>

  <p>AI is really good at recognizing patterns in language. If you ask ChatGPT to write a heartfelt birthday message, it knows the kinds of things people usually say. It'll probably mention how grateful you are for the friendship, how much the person means to you, and how excited you are for another year together.</p>

  <p>And there's nothing necessarily wrong with any of that.</p>

  <p>But AI doesn't actually have the memories or experiences behind your friendship. It can put together words that sound meaningful without having lived through the moments that make those words personal.</p>

<h2>It's literally just a period... right?</h2>

<p>Imagine you text your friend asking if they're still coming over, and they respond with one word:</p>

<p><strong>"Sure."</strong></p>

<p>Now imagine they respond with:</p>

<p><strong>"sureeee!!"</strong></p>

<p>Technically, both messages communicate the same answer. Your friend is coming over. But they probably don't feel the same to you.</p>

<p>The first one might make you wonder if they're annoyed. The second one sounds a lot more excited. Neither interpretation is guaranteed to be right, but we still make those assumptions based on punctuation, spelling, and tone.</p>

<p>This is semiotics in everyday life. We attach meaning to tiny details because of what we've learned through our relationships, experiences, and culture.</p>

<p>And this is where AI gets interesting. If you ask ChatGPT to make a text sound friendlier, it might add exclamation points, change the wording, or throw in an emoji. It recognizes the patterns we associate with friendliness.</p>

<p>But making something sound friendly isn't necessarily the same as actually feeling that way.</p>

<p><strong>The question is:</strong> If AI can copy the signs we associate with emotion, how do we decide what's genuine?</p>

  <h2>When everyone starts sounding the same</h2>

  <p>I think this matters professionally, too. AI can make an email sound more professional or help someone communicate more clearly. But if everyone starts using the same tools to write everything, I wonder whether our individual voices will start sounding more alike.</p>

  <p>Being a good communicator doesn't always mean sounding perfect. Sometimes the things that make our messages a little different are exactly what make them feel human.</p>

  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The takeaway:</strong>
    AI can recognize the patterns that make a message sound meaningful, but the people receiving it bring their own memories, relationships, and experiences to what it means.
  </div>

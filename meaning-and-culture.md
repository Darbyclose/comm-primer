This page focuses on semiotics: the study of how signs produce meaning. Instead of asking only whether a message arrived, ask what its words, tone, formatting, and interface cues suggest to different audiences.

## What semiotics adds

- Meanings are socially produced, not fixed once and for all.
- The same image, phrase, or interface cue can mean different things in different contexts.
- Cultural codes shape interpretation before explicit argument even begins.

## Read the signs in “professional”

Words such as *accountability*, *expectations*, and *going forward* can sound responsible to one reader and threatening to another. Their meaning comes from workplace history and cultural codes, not from the dictionary alone. AI systems often reproduce familiar professional patterns because those patterns are common in their training data.

Before sending, circle the phrases that classify the other person. Replace abstract judgments with observable events, impact, and a question.

## Why this matters now

In AI-mediated communication, symbols are generated, remixed, and circulated quickly. Semiotics helps explain why misunderstanding can grow even when message delivery is technically successful.

Use this page to map sign systems, connotations, and cultural assumptions in your own AI-assisted message.

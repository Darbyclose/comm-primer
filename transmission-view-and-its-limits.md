The transmission model treats communication as a message moving from a sender through a channel to a receiver. That model is useful—but it is a first check, not a complete explanation.

## What this lens illuminates

- Where messages are lost, distorted, or delayed.
- How channel choice changes reliability.
- Why redundancy and feedback can improve clarity.

## Apply it to the AI message

Jordan’s prompt is a compressed channel: it carries little information about the history between Jordan and Sam. The AI adds another stage of encoding, and the workplace chat removes vocal tone and immediate repair. The message may arrive perfectly while the communication fails.

Ask:

- What context was absent from the prompt?
- What did the AI change besides grammar?
- Is this a message that needs a richer channel, such as a conversation?
- Where can the receiver ask for clarification?

## What critique adds

A humanist critique reminds us that communication is not just signal transfer. People interpret messages through context, relationships, values, and history. The mathematical model is powerful for structure, but incomplete for lived communication.

Use transmission theory to locate breakdowns, then move to the other lenses to explain why a technically successful message can still hurt.

This page applies Stuart Hall's encoding/decoding framework to the politics of meaning. It asks who gets to encode dominant interpretations and how audiences negotiate or resist them.

## Core idea

Meaning is not guaranteed by the producer. Audiences can read messages in dominant, negotiated, or oppositional ways, depending on social position and context.

## Power dimension

- Platforms, institutions, and media systems shape what becomes "common sense."
- Technical systems and moderation rules can privilege some interpretations over others.
- AI-generated content can amplify dominant framings while obscuring alternatives.

Use this page to examine how authority, visibility, and platform governance influence interpretation.

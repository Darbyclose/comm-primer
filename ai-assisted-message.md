## A familiar scene

Jordan needs to tell a teammate, Sam, that a project is behind schedule. Jordan gives an AI writing assistant a short prompt: “Write a professional message asking Sam to fix the missed deadlines.” The assistant produces:

> Your repeated failure to meet agreed deadlines is affecting the team. Going forward, you are expected to demonstrate greater accountability and submit all work on time.

Jordan sends it with minor edits. Sam hears an accusation, not an invitation to solve a problem. The conversation shifts from the project to the relationship: *Do you think I am irresponsible? Why did you talk to me like that?*

Jordan may have intended efficiency. Sam may have experienced judgment. The AI did not invent the workplace hierarchy, but it helped give that hierarchy a voice.

## A theory-based solution

Use the **human-in-the-loop communication protocol** before sending an AI-assisted message:

1. **Name the human goal.** Write what you want the other person to understand, feel, and be able to do.
2. **Mark the situation.** Add the relationship, history, stakes, and cultural context that a prompt may leave out.
3. **Generate options, not a verdict.** Ask for two or three versions with different levels of directness. Treat them as drafts, not neutral translations.
4. **Read for reception.** Imagine how the recipient could decode the message from their position, not only how the sender intended it.
5. **Restore dialogue.** Include a specific question or invitation to respond, and choose a channel suited to the emotional stakes.

For Jordan, a better message might be:

> I want to check in about the last two deadlines. I’m concerned that the delays are putting pressure on the rest of the team, and I want to understand what is getting in the way. Can we talk today about what support or timeline would make the next step realistic?

This version is still clear about the problem. It also leaves room for information, dignity, and repair.

## What the lenses reveal

| Lens | Question to ask | Risk it catches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmission | What could be missing, distorted, or misunderstood? | The prompt leaves out context. |
| Meaning and culture | What do words such as “accountability” signal here? | A professional code feels like a personal judgment. |
| Interpretation and power | Who has the authority to label one tone “professional”? | The message makes hierarchy sound natural. |
| Interpretation and intention | What did the sender mean, what does the text support, and what did the system optimize? | Intent becomes an excuse to ignore impact. |

The solution is not to ban AI writing tools. It is to refuse the idea that fluent output is neutral communication.

<div class="callout" role="note" aria-label="Key takeaway">
**A message is ready when a person has reviewed its likely meaning—not merely when an AI has improved its grammar.**
</div>
